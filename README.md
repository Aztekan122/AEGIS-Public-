## Whole-System Intelligence, Governance and Security Architecture

**Developed by Genesis Systems International (GSI)**

AEGIS is a software architecture under active development, designed to support the observation, analysis, governance and security assessment of interconnected systems.

Its objective is to help organisations understand how systems behave, how changes affect them, where dependencies create risk, and what evidence supports an assessment.

> AEGIS does not seek perfect knowledge. AEGIS seeks to continuously reduce uncertainty.

## The Governing Distinction

AEGIS is built around four states of knowledge:

- **Known** — information already established.
- **Observed** — information captured from a system or its environment.
- **Verified** — information that has undergone a defined verification process.
- **Unknown** — information that remains unestablished or uncertain.

An assumption must not silently become an observation. Recorded integrity must not be confused with proof that an external source is authentic.

## Architectural Foundations

The current development architecture includes six principal analytical engines:

| Engine | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Observation | Captures and compares supplied system states. |
| Trust | Supports trust and risk scoring across paths and relationships. |
| Trace | Examines paths and relationships associated with an incident. |
| Impact | Assesses potential impact and blast radius. |
| Simulation | Supports incident scenario analysis. |
| Policy | Evaluates defined policies against supplied information. |

These components are brought together through an analytical pipeline.

AEGIS also includes developing capabilities for evidence records, provenance assessment, persistent memory, reflection and audit recording.

## Evidence and Accountability

AEGIS is being developed with an emphasis on:

- Evidence integrity.
- Traceable corrections and record history.
- Provenance assessment.
- Explicit treatment of missing information.
- Separation of recorded integrity from external source authentication.
- Auditable analytical outputs.

Corrections should preserve their relationship with the original record rather than silently replacing historical information.

## Governing Principles

1. Observation before assumption.
2. Evidence before confidence.
3. Unknown information remains visible.
4. The map assists navigation but must never replace observation.
5. Preparedness comes from understanding change.
6. Growth is desirable; stability is essential.
7. Expansion should build upon verified foundations.

The objective is not unrestricted expansion. The objective is trusted expansion.

## Intended Application Domains

Potential application domains include:

- Cybersecurity and infrastructure assurance.
- Interconnected enterprise and technology systems.
- Third-party and supply-chain dependency assessment.
- Critical and sovereign infrastructure.
- AI and autonomous technology governance.
- Incident analysis and operational resilience.

These are intended domains, not claims that every capability is already implemented or validated.

## Operational Philosophy

The broader GSI approach follows a continuous assurance cycle:

**Assess → Secure → Verify → Reassess**
